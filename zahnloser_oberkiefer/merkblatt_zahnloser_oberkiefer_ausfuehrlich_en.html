Edentulous Upper Jaw
Implant treatment in an edentulous upper jaw is an overall concept. Bone, sinus anatomy, lip support, cleaning ability, provisional denture and the final prosthetic goal all matter.

Possible treatment goals

Options include a removable implant-stabilized denture, a bar or locator solution, or a fixed restoration in selected situations.

Fixed is not automatically better. Lip support, speech, cleaning, repairability, costs and prognosis must be realistic.

Why the upper jaw is special

The posterior upper jaw is often limited by the maxillary sinus. Bone quality can be softer than in the lower jaw, and bone augmentation or sinus lift may be needed.

Planning steps

The final tooth position is planned first. From this, implant number, implant distribution, bone augmentation and provisional denture adjustment are derived.

Risks and possible complications

RiskWhat it means
Sinus lift / sinus symptomsOften relevant in the posterior upper jaw.
Augmentation exposure or lossMore likely with larger reconstructions.
Implant lossRisk increases with smoking, diabetes, overload or poor bone quality.
Prosthetic compromiseLip support, cleaning and maintenance may limit what is sensible.

Aftercare

  • Wear the denture only as instructed and pressure-free.
  • Attend controls and denture adjustment appointments.
  • Avoid smoking and overloading the implants.

Frequently asked questions

Is fixed always possible?

No. Bone, lip support, cleaning ability, costs and prognosis decide.

Do I always need a sinus lift?

Not always, but the sinus is often relevant in the posterior upper jaw.

Can I receive immediate fixed teeth?

Only in selected cases with suitable stability and prosthetic planning.
