Implant Uncovering
Implant uncovering is the small second-stage procedure after a covered implant has healed. The implant is made accessible and a healing cap or gingiva former is attached.

Why uncovering is needed

Many implants heal underneath the gum. Before the crown, bridge or denture can be made, the gum must be opened and shaped around the implant.

Techniques

Depending on the situation, uncovering may be performed by punch excision, flap uncovering or soft tissue management such as vestibuloplasty.

What happens next

After soft tissue healing, your general dentist can take an impression or scan and start the prosthetic restoration.

Typical timeline

Anaesthesia

Local anaesthesia is usually sufficient.

Uncovering

The implant cover screw is exposed.

Healing cap

A healing cap or gingiva former is attached.

Healing

Soft tissue heals for several weeks.

Prosthetics

Impression/scan and crown or denture planning.

Risks and possible complications

RiskWhat it means
Bleeding / swellingUsually mild and temporary.
Loose healing capCan happen and should be checked; do not swallow small components.
Wound healing problemMore relevant with smoking, diabetes or pressure from dentures.
Soft tissue deficitMay require additional soft tissue management.

Aftercare

  • Keep the area clean but do not scrub the wound.
  • Avoid pressure from provisional restorations.
  • Attend controls and the prosthetic follow-up appointment.

Frequently asked questions

Is this a large operation?

Usually no. It is typically a short second-stage procedure.

When can the crown be made?

Usually after soft tissue healing, often after several weeks.

What if the healing cap becomes loose?

Contact the practice so it can be checked and tightened or replaced.
