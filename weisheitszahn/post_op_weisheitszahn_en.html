After Wisdom Tooth Surgery

Postoperative instructions for home
Please read this page once completely on the day of surgery. It does not replace the personal instructions given after the procedure, but it summarizes the most important behavior rules, medication, and warning signs.
1 · The first hours
Rest and positioning

Give yourself complete rest today. Lie down, but keep your upper body elevated, for example with two pillows. Avoid physical exertion, bending down, heavy lifting, and anything that strongly stimulates your circulation.

Cooling: damp, mild, in intervals

Cool the cheeks from the outside with a damp, cool cloth or bandage. Cool water at about 17 °C is ideal, but no ice. Cool for about 10 minutes at a time, then pause for 10 minutes.

Why no ice pack? Excessive cold can overcool the tissue. After it is removed, the body tries to warm the area again quickly. This can increase blood flow and may promote swelling, bruising, pain, and postoperative bleeding.
Gauze and bleeding

If gauze was placed, bite on it constantly with pressure, usually for 30 to 60 minutes. Please do not keep checking in between, because this repeatedly interrupts clotting.

Normal: Pink-colored saliva or a slight taste of blood on the day of surgery is common. What matters is whether fresh blood continues to run.
2 · Getting home, traffic, and collection
After local anesthesia

Circulation problems can occur even after treatment under local anesthesia. Do not drive a car or ride a bicycle yourself if you feel unsure, shaky, or strained. Walking home, taking a taxi, or being a passenger is safer.

After sedation or general anesthesia

After oral tablet sedation, twilight sedation, or general anesthesia, you are not fit to use the road. You must not drive a car or ride a bicycle yourself, and you must not leave the practice alone.

Collection from the practice is mandatory: The accompanying person collects you in the practice. The easiest option is parking in the Carree parking garage and then using the elevator route to the car. If a car is not possible, we recommend going home by taxi with an accompanying person. Public transport is not suitable after sedation or general anesthesia.

For 24 hours after sedation or general anesthesia, please do not operate machinery, do not drink alcohol, and do not make important legal or financial decisions.

3 · Eating and drinking
Today
  • Eat only once the numbness has completely worn off.
  • Cool or lukewarm soft food: yogurt, pudding, cold soup, apple sauce, smoothie.
  • No hot, hard, or crumbly foods, for example rolls, chips, nuts.
  • Drink enough, but only cold or lukewarm drinks.
  • Do not drink through a straw or a sports bottle with suction.
Days 1 to 7
  • Soft food: mashed potatoes, soups, soft pasta, scrambled eggs, smoothies.
  • Nothing spicy, no chili, no pepper.
  • Nothing hard or crunchy, so the wound is not irritated or opened.
  • Plan for sufficient energy and fluid intake.
Important: Too little eating and drinking increases the risk of circulation problems, nausea, vomiting, poorer pain-medication tolerance, stronger pain, and wound-healing problems.
If you feel nauseous: First take small sips of water and some soft food. Please do not take pain medication on an empty stomach. If you vomit repeatedly or cannot keep fluids and medication down, please contact us.
4 · Stimulants and oral hygiene
Clear pause

For at least 3 to 5 days: no alcohol, no nicotine, no coffee, no energy drinks. These substances can promote postoperative bleeding and delay wound healing. Not smoking is especially important because smoking significantly increases the risk of dry socket.

Brushing teeth and protecting the blood clot

Continue brushing all other teeth carefully, but avoid the surgical area. Today, please do not rinse vigorously, do not gargle, do not suck on the wound, and do not spit saliva out forcefully. When rinsing, simply let fluid run out of your mouth.

From tomorrow, you may gently move an alcohol-free mouth rinse or, if recommended, chlorhexidine 0.2% around the mouth twice daily after brushing for about 1 minute and then let it run out of your mouth.

Salt-water mouth bath from the following day: As an alternative or addition, you can briefly hold lukewarm salt water in your mouth after eating or move it only gently. Do not gargle and do not rinse with pressure.
No oral irrigator: Do not use an oral irrigator and do not use a rinsing syringe unless we have explicitly shown it to you or given it to you.
5 · Medication
Pain medication

Take the prescribed pain medication as discussed with you. If you have already received a prescription, the first tablet can ideally be taken about 1 hour before the procedure; otherwise take a tablet soon after the procedure, even if the local anesthetic is still working.

On the day of surgery, take the pain medication as needed and often also in the evening before going to sleep. From the next day onward, take it as needed according to the plan. Please do not take pain medication on an empty stomach. Pain can increase during the first 2 to 3 days; this is often normal.

Antibiotic and mouth rinse

If an antibiotic was prescribed, take it according to our recommendation and do not stop it on your own.

A mouth rinse can support oral hygiene. It usually starts the day after the procedure, twice daily after brushing, gently moving it around for about 1 minute and then letting it run out of your mouth.

No aspirin / ASA: Please avoid it because it has a blood-thinning effect and can promote postoperative bleeding.
6 · What is normal?
2-3
days

Swelling and pain can be strongest on the 2nd or 3rd day and then slowly subside.

3-6
hours

The numbness usually wears off within a few hours. As long as lips, cheek, or tongue are numb, please do not chew.

1-2
weeks

Mouth opening may be limited. This usually improves step by step.

38.5
degrees

Mild fever up to 38.5 °C during the first 2 days can occur as an immune reaction.

Bruise
mark

A bruise on the jaw or neck may look noticeable, but is usually harmless and disappears on its own.

Suture
check

Sutures are usually removed after about 1 week, unless dissolvable sutures were used.

7 · Warning signs
Please contact us promptly in case of
  • heavy bleeding that does not stop despite 30 minutes of pressure on gauze or a clean cloth handkerchief
  • pain that clearly increases again from day 3 to 4 after initially improving
  • deep-seated pain or pain radiating to the ear, bad taste or smell despite pain medication
  • fever above 38.5 °C or fever lasting longer than 2 days
  • pus, very unpleasant taste, or very foul smell
  • numbness of lip, chin, or tongue that is still present the next day
Heavy bleeding: immediate measure

Bite down with constant pressure on a clean cloth handkerchief or gauze bandage for 30 minutes. Keep your head elevated. Do not keep checking, otherwise the bleeding starts again repeatedly.

8 · Special rules for upper jaw and maxillary sinus
Only if we have told you so

If an upper wisdom tooth was very close to the maxillary sinus or if we gave you maxillary sinus rules after the procedure, additional precautions apply for 10 to 14 days.

Avoid pressure
  • Do not blow your nose; only wipe it carefully.
  • Keep your mouth open when sneezing.
  • No pressure equalization, no diving, and no strong straining/pressing breathing.
  • If fluid comes out of your nose when drinking: please contact us.
9 · Work, sport, and rest
Sick leave

The duration depends on the extent of the procedure and your occupation. For office work, 2 to 3 days are often sufficient; for physical work, more like 5 to 7 days.

Sport and exertion

No sport, sauna, swimming pool, or heavy lifting for 10 to 14 days. Exertion increases blood pressure and can trigger postoperative bleeding.

General rest

Avoid anything that makes you sweat or strongly strains your circulation. Deliberately plan the first few days calmly.

Key sentence for home

Rest, damp mild cooling, do not constantly interrupt gauze pressure, eat and drink enough, no suction at the wound, medication as discussed, and contact us immediately in case of warning signs.
