Bone Augmentation Techniques
Bone augmentation techniques differ because bone defects differ. Small contour defects, horizontal defects and vertical defects need different forms of stability and protection.

Basic principles

Bone regeneration needs space, stability, blood supply and protection from pressure or infection.

Autologous means your own bone. Allogeneic means processed donor bone. Alloplastic means synthetic material. These terms must not be confused.

Techniques overview

Sticky bone combines particulate material with blood concentrates or similar concepts to improve handling in smaller or moderate defects.

Semilunar technique, shell technique, allogeneic cortical plates and titanium-reinforced PTFE membranes create more form-stable spaces for larger defects.

How the decision is made

The decision depends on DVT/CBCT findings, defect shape, implant position, soft tissue, provisional restoration and risk factors such as smoking or diabetes.

Typical timeline

Diagnosis

Clinical examination and imaging.

Technique choice

Particulate graft, shell, plate, membrane or combination.

Augmentation

The graft is stabilized and protected.

Healing

Several months, depending on the extent.

Implant planning

Simultaneous or staged implant placement.

Frequently asked questions

Is sticky bone a major augmentation?

Usually it is a particulate approach for small to moderate defects or as an adjunct.

Is allogeneic the same as synthetic?

No. Allogeneic is processed donor bone; synthetic material is alloplastic.

Why use a titanium-reinforced PTFE membrane?

It keeps the regenerative space open and protects the graft.
