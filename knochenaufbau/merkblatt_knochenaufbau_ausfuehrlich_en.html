Bone Augmentation
Bone Augmentation
Bone augmentation is performed when the jawbone is not wide or high enough for a stable implant position. The aim is to create a predictable bone bed for later or simultaneous implant placement.

Why bone can be missing

After tooth loss, inflammation or periodontitis, the jaw ridge can lose width and height. An implant needs stable bone around it.

Common methods

Depending on the defect, we may use autologous bone chips, bone substitute material, membranes, shell technique or a combination.

Small defects can often be augmented at the same time as implant placement. Larger defects may need a separate healing phase.

Healing

Bone augmentation takes time. Early loading, smoking or pressure from dentures can increase the risk of wound problems and volume loss.

Typical timeline

Planning

Clinical examination, imaging and risk assessment.

Augmentation

Bone material is placed, stabilized and protected.

Healing

Several months depending on the size of the defect.

Implant

Simultaneous or staged implant placement.

Restoration

Final prosthetic work after implant healing.

Aftercare

  • Rest and cool gently after surgery.
  • Do not smoke.
  • Avoid pressure from dentures or provisional restorations.
  • Use medication and mouth rinse only as instructed.

Frequently asked questions

Can bone augmentation be done with the implant?

Sometimes yes, especially for smaller defects. Larger defects may require staged treatment.

Does bone grafting hurt?

The procedure is performed under anaesthesia. Postoperative swelling and soreness are expected but usually manageable.

When can the implant be placed?

That depends on the defect and method. Several months of healing may be needed.
