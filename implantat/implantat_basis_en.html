Dental Implant Basics
A dental implant replaces a missing tooth root. It is placed in the jawbone and later serves as the foundation for a crown, bridge or denture. The visible prosthetic restoration is usually planned and made by your general dentist.

What is an implant?

A dental implant is an artificial tooth root, most often a small titanium screw. It heals firmly into the jawbone and can then support the visible restoration.

The implant itself is not the crown. It is the foundation in the bone. The crown, bridge or denture is later attached to it.

What can implants be used for?

A single implant can replace one missing tooth without grinding down healthy neighbouring teeth.

Several implants can support a fixed bridge or stabilize a removable denture. In an edentulous jaw, implants can improve chewing, speaking and confidence.

Normal treatment pathway

After a tooth extraction, an interval of about 8 weeks before implant planning is often useful. A longer interval is usually not a problem.

Planning starts with the desired tooth position. From there we determine the implant position, the need for bone augmentation and the surgical pathway.

The initial implant consultation includes a detailed medical history, the necessary examinations and a detailed discussion of possible treatment concepts and alternatives. This implant consultation is not covered by statutory health insurance; depending on the effort and necessary examinations, costs can be up to EUR 150.

When does it become more complex?

If bone width or bone height is insufficient, bone augmentation, sinus lift, staged treatment or another prosthetic solution may be needed.

A 3D scan (DVT/CBCT) is useful when bone, nerve, sinus or implant position must be assessed in three dimensions.

Typical timeline

Consultation

Examination, goals, medical history and available X-rays.

Backward planning

The final tooth position guides the implant position.

Implant placement

The implant is placed under local anaesthesia or an individually planned anaesthesia option.

Healing

Usually about 3-4 months; longer if bone augmentation is required.

Prosthetics

Uncovering, impression/scan and final restoration by your general dentist.

Aftercare

  • Do not load the implant area during healing.
  • Stop or reduce smoking as much as possible.
  • Attend all control appointments and implant recall visits.
  • Plan the final restoration only after surgical clearance.

Frequently asked questions

Is an implant a complete tooth?

No. The implant replaces the tooth root. The visible crown or denture is added later.

Do I always need 3D imaging?

Not always. It is used when bone, nerve, sinus or implant position must be assessed more precisely.

Am I too old for an implant?

Age alone is usually not the key factor. General health, medication, bone, cleaning ability and treatment goal matter more.
