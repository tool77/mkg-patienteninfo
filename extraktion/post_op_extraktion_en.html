After Tooth Extraction

Postoperative instructions for home
Please read this page once completely on the day of surgery. It does not replace the personal instructions given after your procedure, but it summarizes the most important rules, medication advice and warning signs after a tooth extraction.
1 · The First Hours
Rest and Position

Rest today. Keep your upper body elevated, for example with two pillows. Avoid physical exertion, bending down, heavy lifting and anything that strongly increases your circulation.

Gauze and Bleeding

If gauze was placed, bite down with steady pressure, usually for 30 to 60 minutes. Please do not keep checking in between, because this repeatedly interrupts clot formation.

Normal: Pink saliva or a slight taste of blood on the day of surgery is common. The important question is whether fresh blood keeps flowing.
Cooling

If swelling occurs or the extraction was surgical, cool from the outside with a damp cool cloth. Do not place ice directly on the skin. Cool for about 10 minutes, then pause for 10 minutes.

2 · Getting Home, Driving and Escort
After Local Anaesthesia

Even after local anaesthesia, circulatory weakness can occur. Do not drive a car or bicycle yourself if you feel unsure, shaky or unwell. Walking home, taking a taxi or being driven by someone else is safer.

After Sedation or General Anaesthesia

After oral tablet sedation, twilight sedation or general anaesthesia, you are not fit to drive. You must not drive a car or bicycle yourself and must not leave the practice alone.

Pickup from the practice is mandatory: Your accompanying person must collect you from the practice. If a private car is not possible, we recommend going home by taxi with your accompanying person. Public transport is not suitable after sedation or general anaesthesia.

For 24 hours after sedation or general anaesthesia, please do not operate machinery, drink alcohol or make important legal or financial decisions.

3 · Eating and Drinking
Today
  • Eat only after the numbness has completely worn off.
  • Choose cool or lukewarm soft foods: yoghurt, pudding, soup, apple sauce, mashed potatoes.
  • Avoid hot, hard, spicy or crumbly foods.
  • Drink enough, but only cold or lukewarm fluids.
  • Do not drink through a straw or a sports bottle that creates suction.
Over the Next Days
  • Keep to soft foods while the wound is sensitive.
  • Do not chew directly on the extraction wound.
  • Small meals are better than eating nothing.
  • Plan enough energy and fluids.
Important: Too little food and fluid increases the risk of circulatory problems, nausea, vomiting, poorer tolerance of pain medication, stronger pain and delayed wound healing.
If you feel nauseous: Start with small sips of water and a little soft food. Please do not take pain medication on an empty stomach. If you vomit repeatedly or cannot keep fluids and medication down, please contact us.
4 · Protect the Blood Clot
What to Avoid

The blood clot in the wound is the natural wound dressing. It protects the bone and is essential for normal healing.

  • Do not rinse vigorously or gargle.
  • Do not spit forcefully; let saliva run gently out of your mouth.
  • Do not suck on the wound or touch it with your tongue.
  • No straws and no sports bottles that create suction.
Pause Stimulants

For at least 3 to 5 days: no alcohol, no nicotine, no coffee, no energy drinks. These substances can promote bleeding and delay wound healing. Avoiding smoking is especially important because smoking significantly increases the risk of a dry socket.

5 · Oral Hygiene and Medication
Brushing and Rinsing

Continue to brush all other teeth carefully, but avoid the extraction wound at first. On the day of surgery, please do not rinse forcefully. From the next day, you may gently move an alcohol-free mouth rinse or lukewarm salt-water mouth bath around your mouth and then let it run out.

No oral irrigator: Do not use an oral irrigator or rinsing syringe unless we have explicitly shown it to you or given it to you.
Pain Medication and Antibiotics

Take pain medication as discussed with you. Please do not take pain medication on an empty stomach. If an antibiotic was prescribed, take it according to our recommendation and do not stop it on your own.

No Aspirin / ASA: Please avoid it because it thins the blood and can promote postoperative bleeding.
6 · What Is Normal?
2-6
Hours

The numbness usually wears off within a few hours. As long as your lip, cheek or tongue is numb, please do not chew.

1-2
Days

A sore wound, mild swelling and a slight taste of blood can occur.

2-5
Days

A dry socket typically becomes noticeable during this period with increasing pain.

7-10
Days

If sutures were placed, they are usually removed during this period.

2-6
Weeks

The soft tissue gradually becomes more stable.

3-6
Months

The bone continues to fill in depending on the size of the defect.

7 · Warning Signs
Please Contact Us Promptly If
  • heavy bleeding does not stop despite 30 minutes of pressure on gauze or a clean cloth handkerchief
  • pain clearly increases after initial improvement from day 2 to 5
  • deep pain or pain radiating to the ear occurs, with bad taste or smell despite pain medication
  • fever above 38.5 °C occurs or fever lasts longer than 2 days
  • pus, a very bad taste or a strongly foul smell occurs
  • numbness of the lip, chin or tongue is still present the next day
Heavy Bleeding: Immediate Step

Bite down for 30 minutes with steady pressure on a clean cloth handkerchief or gauze. Keep your head elevated. Do not keep checking, otherwise bleeding may start again repeatedly.

Important if you take blood thinners: If bleeding does not stop despite correct pressure or starts heavily again, please arrange prompt medical or dental assessment.
For problems after the procedure, first use our postoperative emergency number. If we cannot be reached: dental emergency service 01805 60 70 11. Only in a life-threatening emergency, shortness of breath, severe swallowing difficulty or rapid swelling of the floor of the mouth, neck or tongue: call 112 immediately.
8 · Special Rules for the Upper Jaw and Maxillary Sinus
Only If We Told You So

If an upper posterior tooth was very close to the maxillary sinus, or if we gave you sinus precautions after the procedure, additional precautions apply for 10 to 14 days.

Avoid Pressure
  • Do not blow your nose; wipe it gently only.
  • Keep your mouth open when sneezing.
  • No pressure equalization, no diving and no forceful breathing against resistance.
  • If fluid comes out of your nose when drinking, please contact us.
9 · Work, Sports and Rest
Sick Leave

The duration depends on the extent of the procedure and your occupation. After a simple extraction, only a short rest period is often needed; after surgical removal or physically demanding work, several days may be more appropriate.

Sports and Exertion

No sports, sauna, swimming pool or heavy lifting for at least 3 to 7 days. After surgical removal or postoperative bleeding, rest for longer.

General Rest

Avoid anything that makes you sweat or heavily strains your circulation. Keep the day of surgery quiet and increase activity gradually depending on how symptoms develop.

10 · Contact and Key Message
How to Reach Us

Practice: 06151 786540. After the procedure, you will receive our postoperative emergency number. If we cannot be reached: dental emergency service 01805 60 70 11. Only in a life-threatening emergency, shortness of breath, severe swallowing difficulty or rapid swelling: 112.

Key Message for Home

Rest, do not keep interrupting gauze pressure, avoid suction on the wound, eat and drink enough, take medication as discussed and contact us immediately if warning signs occur.
